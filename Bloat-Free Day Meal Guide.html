Why we bloat
(and why it's almost
never serious).

Bloating is your digestive system telling you something's a bit off, not a sign that something's wrong with you. The most common causes are completely fixable.
01

Eating too quickly

When you eat fast, you swallow air and that air has to go somewhere. Slowing down at meals (and chewing properly) is one of the single most effective bloat fixes there is.

02

Not enough water

Mild dehydration slows digestion and causes the body to retain water, which paradoxically makes you feel more bloated, not less. A glass of warm lemon water on rising changes this fast.

03

Too much salt & processed food

Salty foods pull water into your tissues. One takeaway or salty meal can leave you feeling puffy for 24 to 48 hours afterwards.

04

Eating late or close to bed

Your digestion slows down in the evening. A heavy late meal often shows up as a bloated, uncomfortable morning.

05

Hidden food sensitivities

Dairy, certain breads, sugar alcohols (sweeteners), and onions or garlic in large amounts are the most common culprits. We're not cutting them out forever, just for one day, to let your gut settle.

The Bloat-Free Day,
hour by hour.

Here's the rhythm. The next pages have full meal ideas. This is the structure to come back to anytime you need a reset.

On Waking

Warm lemon water

Wakes up your digestion gently, kickstarts bowel movements, rehydrates you after the night. Before coffee. Before anything else.

Breakfast

Within 1 hr of waking, a protein-led meal

Skip the pastries today. Eggs (scrambled with avocado on toast), smoked salmon, or a protein-led smoothie. Eating within an hour of waking helps stabilise blood sugar early, which sets the tone for cravings, energy, and snacking for the rest of the day.

Mid-Morning

Only if you're genuinely hungry

Most days you won't need anything between breakfast and lunch. If you do, keep it small. Think a piece of fruit or some raw veg. Berries, papaya, pineapple, or cucumber sticks are all naturally de-bloating. Sip water alongside.

Lunch

Lean protein with vegetables

Always include a protein element: chicken, fish, eggs, or a vegetarian alternative. A protein-based salad or a vegetable-packed soup with a boiled egg on the side both work beautifully on a bloat-free day.

Afternoon

A bigger snack to bridge to dinner

This is the gap that matters. A more substantial snack here prevents the 3pm slump, steadies blood sugar, and stops the biscuit reach later. Think apple slices with nut butter, a small handful of nuts and raisins, 125g Greek yoghurt with fruit, or 2 boiled eggs. Pair with peppermint or fennel tea to keep things calm on the gut.

Dinner

Warm, light, simple

Soup, baked fish, or roasted veg with a portion of protein. Aim to finish at least 2 hours before bed. Then a 10-minute walk if you can.

Throughout the day

Water, water, water

Aim for around 8 glasses. Add lemon, cucumber, or fresh mint if plain water bores you. One milky coffee is fine — keep the rest as tea or water.

Breakfast · No. 02

Avocado &
Scrambled Egg
on Toast.

Serves 1 · 10 minutes

Ingredients

  • 2 eggs
  • 1 tsp unsalted butter
  • 1 slice wholemeal bread
  • ½ avocado, mashed or sliced
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • Pinch of chilli flakes (optional)

Instructions

  1. Crack the eggs into a bowl, season with black pepper, and beat lightly.
  2. Heat a non-stick pan over medium and add half the butter.
  3. Pour in the eggs and stir gently until softly scrambled.
  4. Toast the bread and spread with the remaining butter.
  5. Top with avocado, then pile the scrambled eggs on top.
  6. Finish with extra black pepper and chilli flakes, if using.

Works with any style of egg — fried, boiled, or poached.

Lunch · No. 01

Italian Roasted
Vegetable Tray.

Serves 2 · 40 minutes

Ingredients

  • 4 chicken or turkey sausages, chopped (or pork, beef, or veggie)
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • 1 carrot, sliced
  • ½ courgette, sliced
  • 200g broccoli, cut into small florets
  • 2 tbsp green pesto
  • ½ tsp garlic powder
  • ½ tsp dried oregano or Italian seasoning
  • ½ tsp paprika
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • 2 handfuls of rocket
  • 1 tbsp sunflower seeds

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 200°C.
  2. Place the sausages and vegetables on a large baking tray.
  3. Add the pesto, garlic powder, oregano, paprika and black pepper. Toss to coat everything evenly.
  4. Roast for 30–35 minutes, turning once, until the sausages are cooked through and the vegetables are golden and tender.
  5. Tip onto plates with the rocket leaves and finish with the sunflower seeds.
Batch Tip

Doubles or triples easily. Roasted veg and sausages keep in the fridge for 2–3 days or freeze for up to 2 months. Add the rocket and seeds just before serving so they stay fresh.

Lunch · No. 02

Sheet Pan
Green Soup.

Serves 3 · 45 minutes

Ingredients

  • 1 large leek, trimmed, washed, cut into 2.5cm pieces
  • 2 small (or 1 large) courgette, chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp Italian seasoning
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • 8 tbsp peas (fresh or frozen)
  • 1 low-sodium vegetable stock cube
  • 1 tin coconut milk
  • 150ml water
  • To serve, per portion: 1 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 200°C.
  2. Place the leek and courgette in a casserole dish. Drizzle with olive oil and sprinkle with garlic powder, Italian seasoning and black pepper. Toss to coat.
  3. Roast for 25–30 minutes, until the vegetables are fork-tender.
  4. Remove from the oven and add the peas, stock cube and coconut milk. Cook for a further 10 minutes.
  5. Tip into a blender with the water and blend until smooth (a hand blender works too).
  6. Transfer to a saucepan and heat through, adding more water for your preferred consistency.
  7. Serve hot, topped with chopped parsley.
Add Protein

Always include a portion of protein with your soup — 125g Greek yoghurt, 1–2 boiled eggs, or any meat, fish or vegetarian alternative. Stir through or serve on the side.

Lunch · No. 03

Ham &
Egg Cups.

Makes 6 cups (2–3 per portion) · 30 minutes

Ingredients

  • ½ tsp olive oil
  • 6 slices of ham
  • 4 eggs
  • 15ml milk of your choice
  • ½ spring onion, chopped
  • ½ red pepper, finely diced
  • ½ tsp oregano
  • Pinch of paprika
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • 60g cheese, grated

For the Side Salad

  • A large mix of leaves, tomatoes, cucumber, red onion (whatever you fancy)
  • 1 tsp mayonnaise, salad cream or dressing — or 1 tbsp coleslaw

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 180°C.
  2. Lightly grease a 6-hole muffin tray with the olive oil.
  3. Cut each slice of ham in half and line each muffin hole with two pieces to form a case.
  4. Whisk together the eggs, milk, spring onion, red pepper, oregano, paprika and black pepper.
  5. Pour the mixture evenly into the ham-lined cases.
  6. Top each cup with grated cheese.
  7. Bake for 18–20 minutes, until the egg is set and lightly golden on top.
  8. While they bake, build your salad on a plate and dress lightly.
  9. Let the cups cool slightly, then serve 2–3 per person with the salad.
Make Ahead

Leftovers keep in an airtight container in the fridge for 2–3 days — eat cold or reheat gently. Chicken or turkey deli slices work in place of ham.

Dinner · No. 01

Cod, Baby Potatoes
& Veggies.

Serves 1 · 40 minutes

Ingredients

  • 160g baby potatoes, halved
  • ½ onion, cut into wedges
  • 8 Brussels sprouts, halved or quartered
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • ½ tsp garlic powder
  • ½ tsp mixed herbs
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • 1 skinless cod fillet (approx. 185g)
  • ½ tsp lemon juice
  • 3 tbsp peas (fresh or frozen)

For the Herbed Yoghurt Sauce

  • 1 heaped tbsp full-fat Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tsp lemon juice
  • Pinch of garlic powder
  • ½ tsp mixed herbs
  • Black pepper, to taste

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 180°C fan.
  2. Arrange the potatoes, onion and Brussels sprouts on a roasting tray. Drizzle with olive oil and sprinkle over the garlic powder, mixed herbs and black pepper. Toss to coat. Roast for 25–30 minutes, until tender and slightly caramelised.
  3. Pat the cod dry with kitchen paper, season with black pepper and a squeeze of lemon. Move the vegetables to the side of the tray and place the cod in the centre.
  4. Return to the oven for 10–15 minutes, until the cod is opaque and flakes easily.
  5. While the cod cooks, boil the peas. Mix all the yoghurt sauce ingredients in a small bowl and season to taste.
  6. Plate the potatoes, vegetables and peas. Add the cod and finish with a generous dollop of the herbed yoghurt sauce.

Flexible — swap in carrots, broccoli, or whatever veg you have to hand.

Dinner · No. 02

Spanish Omelette
+ Salad.

Serves 2 · 30 minutes

Ingredients

  • 300g baby potatoes
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • 1 red onion, diced
  • ½ red pepper, diced
  • 4 eggs
  • 60g cheese, grated
  • 1 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped (or ½ tsp dried)
  • ½ tsp Italian seasoning
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • Pinch of chilli flakes (optional)

To Serve

  • A large mixed salad (leaves, tomatoes, peppers, red onion, cucumber)
  • 2 tsp mayonnaise, salad cream or dressing — or 2 tbsp coleslaw

Instructions

  1. Preheat the grill to high (around 220°C), or the oven to 180°C fan.
  2. Boil or steam the potatoes for 8–10 minutes until just tender. Drain, cool slightly, then slice into rounds.
  3. Heat the oil in a non-stick frying pan over medium heat. Add the onion and pepper and cook for 4–5 minutes until softened.
  4. Add the sliced potatoes and gently stir to combine.
  5. Beat the eggs in a bowl and stir in the cheese, parsley, Italian seasoning, black pepper and chilli flakes if using.
  6. Pour the egg mixture over the vegetables. Cook for 3–5 minutes until the base has set.
  7. Slide under the grill for 2–3 minutes until the top is set and golden, or transfer to the oven for 8–10 minutes.
  8. Serve with the salad and a spoonful of mayo, dressing or coleslaw.

Dinner · No. 03

Honey Beef & Sweet
Potato Bowl.

Serves 1 · 30 minutes

Ingredients

  • 160g sweet potato, cubed
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • ½ tsp paprika
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • 100g minced beef (or pork, turkey, chicken — or 75g Quorn mince)
  • 1 tsp low-sodium soy sauce
  • ⅛ tsp chilli powder (optional)
  • 100g full-fat cottage cheese
  • ½ small avocado, sliced (or ¼ of a large)
  • 1 heaped tsp honey

Instructions

  1. Toss the sweet potato cubes with the olive oil, half the paprika, and black pepper.
  2. Air-fry at 200°C for 15–20 minutes, shaking halfway, until golden and tender. (Or roast at 200°C for 25–30 minutes.)
  3. Meanwhile, heat a non-stick pan over medium heat. Add the mince and cook for 5–7 minutes until browned through.
  4. Stir in the soy sauce and chilli powder (if using). Cook for another 1–2 minutes and taste for seasoning.
  5. Build the bowl: sweet potato, spiced mince, cottage cheese, sliced avocado.
  6. Drizzle the honey over the top just before serving.

What to lean into.
What to leave for tomorrow.

This isn't a forever list. It's a one-day list, a soft reset for your gut. Tomorrow, normal life resumes.

Cruciferous veg are fine well-cooked, it's raw that tends to bloat. If a food makes the "leave for tomorrow" list, it doesn't mean it's bad. It just means we're giving your gut one quiet day.

Lean Into Today
  • Eggs, chicken, salmon, white fish, turkey
  • Plain Greek yoghurt (full-fat), small amounts of cheese
  • Berries, pineapple, papaya, kiwi, half a banana
  • Cooked spinach, courgette, carrots, pak choi, peppers
  • Cucumber, cooked tomatoes
  • Sourdough, oats, small portions of sweet potato or rice
  • Well-cooked lentils (in soup or curry)
  • Olive oil, avocado, almonds, chia seeds
  • Ginger, peppermint, fennel, lemon
  • Water, herbal teas, one milky coffee, plain tea
Leave for Tomorrow
  • Fizzy drinks (yes, including the diet ones)
  • Chewing gum and mints with sweeteners
  • Heavy bread, pastries, croissants
  • Raw onions, raw garlic, raw cruciferous veg (broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage)
  • Raw tomatoes if they don't sit well with you
  • Beans, chickpeas (lentils excepted, see above)
  • Crisps, salty snacks, takeaways
  • Alcohol
  • Anything you already know doesn't agree with you
